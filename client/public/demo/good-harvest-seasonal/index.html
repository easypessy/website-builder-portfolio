Good HarvestSeasonal campaign · concept · 2026

A discount feed teaches people to wait.

The shop was posting price cuts three times a week. It worked, in the sense that things sold — and it trained a whole customer base to hold off until something was reduced. On a grocer's margin that is not a marketing problem, it is a survival one.

The replacement is not "stop discounting". It is moving price out of the headline and putting seasonality there instead: what is good this week, what it costs, and what to do with it.

Same week, two feeds

Nothing here costs more to produce than what was being posted before. The stock is the same stock. Only the headline changed.

What "good this week" actually means

The anchor post is three items, every Monday, with the real price next to each. Pick a month to see what it would say — and notice that some months are thin, which is the honest part.

Two discounts a month, and they are posted late

The cap is the mechanism. Without a hard number, "fewer discount posts" lasts about three weeks — the first quiet Tuesday brings one back, and then the rule is gone.

End-of-day reductions still happen in the shop every evening. They are simply not the thing the account is for. A customer who comes at five for a reduced box is welcome; a customer who has learned to only come at five is the problem being solved.

The target is margin per basket, not reach. A feed that halves its impressions and stops training people to wait has done its job.

Concept campaign. It has not been run, so there is no margin, basket-size or engagement data to report and none is implied. Prices shown are illustrative placeholders rather than a real price list, and the growers named are invented. On a live account these would be the shop's actual weekly prices, photographed on the counter the morning they change.
