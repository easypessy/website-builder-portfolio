AsterCampaign architecture · concept · 2026

The funnel was not leaking at the top.

Interest in the development was healthy — the launch generated plenty of registrations. Viewings did not follow. The brief that arrived asked for more traffic.

The numbers said otherwise. People were moving from the advert to the website in normal proportions and then stopping. Between "this looks interesting" and "book a forty-five minute appointment with a sales negotiator" there was nothing at all, and that gap is a cliff, not a step.

So two intermediate commitments were added. Select a stage below.

The journey, as rebuilt

Six stages. Two of them did not exist before this work — they are marked. The stage marked as the leak is where the original journey asked for far more commitment than the visitor had yet been given a reason to make.

Before and after, in one line each

Follow-up depends on what they asked for

A person who downloaded floor plans and a person who booked a call have told you different things. Sending both the same sequence throws away the only signal the campaign generated.

Concept strategy for an invented development. There is no campaign data here — no registration counts, cost per lead, viewing rate or sales — and the proportions in the bars below each stage are illustrative, drawn to show the shape of the argument rather than to report a measurement. The transferable part is the diagnostic order: look at the middle of the journey before buying more traffic for the top of it.
