The questions clients arrive with

Why is profit falling while sales rise?

Usually a pricing or mix problem that has been invisible for two or three quarters. We find it in the numbers you already have.

Can we afford to hire?

A cash-timing question more often than a profit question. We model the months either side of the decision.

Is this contract worth renewing?

We cost it properly, including the time it takes that nobody is billing for.

What is this business actually worth?

A valuation you can defend, with the assumptions written down so a buyer can argue with them.

Who we work with

Owner-managed businesses between roughly ten and ninety staff, usually at the point where the founder can no longer hold the whole picture in their head. Manufacturing, logistics, professional services and food production.

We are a poor fit for pre-revenue startups and for anyone looking for a signature on accounts prepared elsewhere.

What changed, and why

Four structural edits. None of them made the page cleverer — they moved the reader's question in front of the firm's history.

The headline names a problem, not a firm

A visitor arrives mid-worry. The first line should describe the worry, so they know they are in the right place before they decide to keep reading.

Was: "Westcombe Partners — Chartered Advisers Since 1998"

Section headings became questions

Clients do not search for "our service offering". They search for the question they are stuck on. The headings now match the words in their head.

Was: "Our Services" / "Our Approach" / "Our Values"

Credentials moved below the fold and halved

Founding date, office count and qualifications are still there. They now act as reassurance after interest, rather than as a barrier before it.

Was: 180 words of firm history in the opening two paragraphs

The call to action describes the next event

"Book a first conversation" tells the reader what will physically happen. "Get in touch" tells them nothing, so they weigh the risk themselves.

Was: "Get in touch to discuss your requirements"
