The rules

  • Three to seven words. Longer and the diner stops reading at dish four.
  • Name the cooking, not the feeling. "Grilled hard" beats "kissed by flame".
  • Warn where a warning helps. "One bone to watch", "very sweet", "bone in".
  • No origin stories on the menu. The farm goes on the wall, not next to the price.
  • No adjectives the kitchen can't deliver. If it isn't crisp every time, don't write crisp.
  • Share instructions are useful. "Share it" prevents two of the same thing arriving.

Before and after

Same dish, same kitchen.

BEFOREPan-seared Atlantic mackerel fillet, delicately smoked in-house over applewood, served atop a bed of shaved heritage fennel with a burnt citrus gel and micro herbs, finished with a drizzle of first-press olive oil.
AFTERSmoked mackerel — burnt lemon, fennel, cold.

The applewood, the gel and the micro herbs are all still on the plate. The diner finds out when it arrives, which is the better moment.

Specials rule 1

Specials are written by whoever is on the pass, at 4pm, on a card. The rule that keeps them in voice: name the ingredient that changed, and say why it's only tonight.
