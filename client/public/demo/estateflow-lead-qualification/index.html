Four questions, then a decision

Each step has a stated failure path. That is the part most flow diagrams leave out, and the part an agency owner needs to audit before trusting it with enquiries.

01

Budget

Accepts a range, a single figure, or "not sure". A range is banded to the nearest bracket.

If refused twice: pass to nurture, do not press.
02

Area

Matched against the areas the agency actually covers, including common misspellings and informal names.

If outside coverage: polite decline with a reason.
03

Timeline

Sorts into moving now, within six months, or browsing. Browsing is a valid answer, not a failure.

If vague: ask once more, then record as unknown.
04

Finance

Asks whether a mortgage is agreed in principle. Never gives advice, never quotes a rate.

If asked for advice: hand to a human immediately.

What the system is not allowed to do

The constraints matter more than the capabilities. A qualification flow that oversteps costs an agency more than one it under-delivers on.

No valuations

Any question about what a property is worth is routed to a named agent. The flow replies that needs a person and stops.

No price negotiation

Asking price is read from the listing and repeated verbatim. The flow has no authority to discuss movement on it.

No silent decline

An enquiry outside coverage gets a written reason and, where possible, a direction to try instead. It is never dropped.

Honest limits

This is a concept build. It has never handled a real enquiry, so there is no data to quote — no lead volumes, no conversion figures, no time saved.

  • The conversation above is a scripted demonstration, not a live model. Replies are fixed.
  • Banding budgets loses detail. An agency with unusual price brackets would need the bands rewritten.
  • Area matching depends on a maintained list of covered areas. It will fail on a new development with no established name.
  • A qualification flow filters; it does not sell. Every qualified lead still needs the agent to call.
