Autumn — third collectionNineteen pieces, made once.

We release three times a year and then we stop.

Northline is four people in a workshop. We cut in small runs, from mills we have used for six years, and when a run is finished it is not repeated — not as a sales device, but because the cloth was bought once.

That shapes everything here. There is no sale, no countdown, no restock notification. If a coat is gone it is gone, and the next collection will be along in roughly four months.

Each garment page tells you the mill, the weight of the cloth, who cut it and how it will behave after a year. We would rather you bought one thing and kept it than three things and replaced them.

Look 01 — the Harbour coat

Cut long enough to sit down in, which sounds obvious and almost never is.

Undyed Donegal wool at 740gsm, unlined through the body so it moves rather than holds its shape. The pockets are set low and deep because the first version had them at the usual height and nobody used them.

Look 02 — shirting

Four shirts, one pattern, four cloths.

The same block in oxford, brushed cotton, heavy linen and a cotton–linen mix. Buying the same shape in different weights is how most people actually dress, and it is almost impossible to do from a normal shop.

Look 03 — knitwear

Two jumpers. We tried five and three were not good enough.

Lambswool from a spinner in Yorkshire, knitted on old machines that run slowly and produce a denser fabric. They will pill at the cuffs in the first month and then stop. We say so on the page rather than in an email afterwards.

The garments

Price appears once, in the same size as everything else. Select any piece for cloth, fit and the honest note about how it wears.

Four people, one room, three runs a year.

Patterns are cut here. Sewing is done by a workshop twenty minutes away that we have used since the beginning — eleven people, most of whom have been there longer than we have been trading.

We do not describe this as a story. It is simply the production arrangement, and it is the reason a coat takes nine weeks and costs what it costs.

If you want to know where a specific garment was made, ask. We will tell you the workshop, not a region.

Where to find it

Everything is sold here and in two shops. We do not wholesale further than that, because a third account would mean making more than we can cut properly.

Fitting questions are answered by the person who made the pattern, usually the same day. Sizing between two is common on the coats — ask before you order rather than after.
