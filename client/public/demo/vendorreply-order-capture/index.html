Let the system do the transcription, keep the trust decision human

Everything up to payment is clerical work: reading, sorting, copying. Payment is a judgement with money attached, and it is where fraud enters a social-selling business.

  1. A screenshot is not a payment. Edited transfer receipts are common enough that no vendor should accept an image as proof without checking the account.
  2. The system flags, the person decides. The order sits at "payment unverified" until a human opens the banking app and confirms the money arrived.
  3. No goods move on a flag. Packing and dispatch are blocked at that state, so the checkpoint cannot be skipped by accident on a busy day.
  4. The delay is honest with the customer. The reply says the order is held until payment clears, rather than implying it is already confirmed.

Honest limits

Concept workflow. It has not processed a real order, so there are no accuracy rates, time savings or error reductions to report.

  • Extraction from casual chat is imperfect. Voice notes, photos of handwriting and mixed-language messages are the common failure cases.
  • An address given as "the usual place" cannot be resolved without order history, and will always be asked for again.
  • The payment checkpoint is deliberate friction. A vendor who finds it slow will be tempted to remove it, which removes the point of the build.
  • Instagram's messaging rules change. Anything depending on their interface needs maintenance, not just a launch.
