People buy an area first and a building second.

Every agency posts the same carousel — five rooms, a price, a phone number. It collects saves from other agents and almost nothing from buyers. This direction moves the subject off the property and onto the street it sits on.

One listing in four

Sixteen posts, four weeks. Listings are capped at a quarter of the feed — not because listings are bad, but because an account that is only listings has nothing to offer anyone who is not buying this month. Select any square.

The guide format, fixed

Every neighbourhood guide is the same six stops in the same order, which is what makes it a format rather than a series of one-offs. It is written as a walk because that is how a buyer will actually experience the area on viewing day. Scroll sideways.

The costs nobody mentions

A recurring series answering the questions a first-time buyer is slightly embarrassed to ask an agent. It is the least glamorous content in the plan and the reason someone follows the account eight months before they buy anything.

01What do I actually need on the day I exchange?

The deposit, usually ten per cent, has to be with your solicitor as cleared funds before exchange — not on the day. Cleared means it has sat in the account, which for most banks is two to three working days. This is the single most common reason a chain slips by a week.

02Why is the service charge different on two flats in the same building?

Usually floor area, sometimes a share apportioned by the original lease rather than by size. Ask for the last two years of accounts, not the current estimate. An estimate is a forecast; the accounts are what happened.

03Is a short lease always a problem?

Under eighty years it gets expensive to extend, because a payment to the freeholder is added on top. Between eighty and ninety it is manageable but should affect the price. Over ninety-five it is rarely the thing to worry about first.

04What does a survey miss?

A standard homebuyer's report does not lift carpets, move furniture or test drains. If the property is older than about 1930, the extra cost of a full building survey is usually smaller than the first thing it finds.

05How long is "sold subject to contract", realistically?

For a chain-free flat with a cash buyer, six to eight weeks. In a chain of three, twelve to sixteen is normal and nobody should be alarmed at week ten. Anyone quoting four weeks is describing a best case.
