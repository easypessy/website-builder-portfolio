Little Lantern · parent content · 2026

Ninety seconds, one hand, and a lot of guilt.

The centre was posting classroom photographs. Parents liked them, engagement looked healthy, and enquiries stayed exactly where they were — because a photograph of a happy classroom answers no question a prospective parent is actually carrying.

So every post now answers one anxiety, briefly, from a member of staff with a name.

What parents actually ask

This is the live format. Filter by age, then open a question — the same way a parent would on a phone, standing up, between two other things.

Four pillars, four worries

Not "educational", "behind the scenes", "testimonials", "fun". Each pillar is a thing a parent lies awake about, which is the only reason they would stop scrolling for it.

01

Is my child behind?

The most common private worry and the one nobody asks aloud at the gate. Answered with ranges rather than benchmarks, because a range is honest and a benchmark is frightening.

RuleNever publish a single "should be able to" figure. Always a range, always with the words "and both are normal".
02

What should reading look like at this age?

Concrete and observable. What a parent would actually see at the kitchen table, not what a curriculum document says.

RuleDescribe behaviour, not levels. No scheme names, no colour bands, no acronyms.
03

What do I do about screens?

The question with the most guilt attached, so the answer carries none. Practical, specific, and free of the implication that the parent has already failed.

RuleNo time limits presented as science. Say what is known, say what is not, and stop.
04

Homework refusal

A behaviour problem dressed as an academic one. Answered by the person who deals with it every week rather than by the centre as an institution.

RuleAlways attributed to a named member of staff. An unsigned answer reads as marketing.

Written to be read one-handed

Format constraints

  • Answer in the first two lines. No preamble, no "great question".
  • Under 120 words. If it needs more, it is two posts.
  • Type large enough to read at arm's length on a phone in daylight.
  • One age band per post, tagged, so parents can skip 80% of the feed guilt-free.
  • No clip-art children, no cartoon pencils, no primary-colour confetti.

Where the questions come from

  • An anonymous box in the reception, emptied monthly.
  • Whatever staff were asked twice at pick-up that week.
  • The questions in enquiry emails that never turned into a visit.
  • Never from a keyword tool. A parent's phrasing and a search engine's phrasing are not the same thing.

On using worry as a hook

Anxiety-led content has an obvious failure mode: manufacture the fear, then sell the cure. The line drawn here is that every post has to leave a parent able to act on their own, without the centre. If the only resolution offered is "book an assessment", it does not go out.

Concept system. It has not run on a live account, so there are no follower, engagement or enquiry figures to report — and none are implied. The staff names below are invented.

What the posts actually look like

Large type, one question per post, a named member of staff, and an age tag so a parent can skip the eighty per cent that is not about their child.
