Local visibility is maintenance, not a launch.

A plan written for an anonymised neighbourhood services business: strong word of mouth, almost no digital presence, and aggregator sites outranking it for its own name. Three things usually run separately — listings, reviews and content — treated here as one weekly routine.

Section one

Where the business stands

The business has traded for eleven years and takes most of its work from recommendation. That is a strength and the reason the gap went unnoticed: nobody searching was ever the source of a job, so nobody missed them.

  • Searching the business name returns two directory aggregators above anything the business controls.
  • Opening hours differ across four platforms. Two still show hours changed three years ago.
  • Fourteen reviews, the most recent eight months old, none responded to.
  • No pages on the site that answer a question anyone types before buying.

The problem is not ranking. The problem is that four platforms disagree about the business, and a search engine resolving that disagreement will trust the aggregator that is consistent over the business that is not.

Section three

Review routine

Reviews stopped because asking was left to whoever remembered. The fix is to attach the request to an event that already happens: the job being finished and paid for.

The loop

  1. Job completed and invoice settled.
  2. Same day, a short message: one sentence of thanks, one direct link, no form.
  3. Every review answered within three working days, including the poor ones.
  4. One sentence from a recent review added to the site each month.

Target the rate, not the total. Roughly one in six people asked will write something, so a business completing twelve jobs a week should expect about two reviews a week — not forty next Tuesday.

Answering a bad one

Acknowledge the specific thing, say what changed, offer to continue off the platform. No defence of the company and no apology template. Future customers read the reply more carefully than the complaint.

Section seven

What this plan cannot promise

This is a concept plan written for a portfolio. It has not been executed, so there is no ranking data, no call volume and no enquiry figure attached to it. Any number on this page describes the plan itself — tasks, weeks, topics — not a result.

Local search position depends on distance from the searcher, competitor activity and platform changes that nobody controls. A business three streets further out can do everything here and still sit below a closer competitor doing less.

The honest expectation is that correcting listings fixes a correctness problem within weeks, reviews compound over months, and content takes a quarter before it is worth judging. A plan that promises faster is guessing.
