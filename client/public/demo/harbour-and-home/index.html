Nobody has ever wanted "three bedrooms".

They want somewhere they can walk to the station from, or room for a workshop, or a street that is quiet on a Sunday. Start there — the bedroom count still works underneath.

Photography policy

Every listing on this site is photographed to the same rules, and the rules are published because the alternative is an industry where a small flat and a large one look identical online.

  • No wide-angle below 24mm. It makes a room look a third larger and it is the single most common reason a viewing is a disappointment.
  • One fixed aspect ratio across every listing. So two properties are compared at the same size, not at whichever crop flattered.
  • Nothing removed in editing. Bins, cables, a neighbouring extension — if it is there when you visit, it is there in the photograph.
  • Every room shown, including the small one. A missing room is always the worst room.
  • Dated. Every photograph carries the month it was taken. Spring light on a December listing is a lie by omission.

How the lifestyle filters work

They are not a separate database. Each one resolves to ordinary structured fields, which is why they can be combined with the normal filters above without anything breaking.

  • Walkable → within 900m of the station or the high street, measured on foot, not as the crow flies.
  • Space to work → floor area above 95m² or an outbuilding flagged on the record.
  • Room to grow → three or more bedrooms and a garden over 40 feet.
  • Quiet street → not a through road, and not within 150m of a main road.
  • Ready to move into → no chain and no work flagged by the last survey we saw.

The buyer speaks their own language. The database hears its own. Neither has to compromise.
